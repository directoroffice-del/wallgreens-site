H20 Timber Beam · Technical Article

The H20 Timber Beam Explained

How the world's standard timber formwork beam works, what makes it EN 13377 certified, and how to specify the right Form-on variant for your project.

What Is an H20 Timber Beam?

The H20 is the international standard timber formwork beam — a 200 mm deep timber I-beam engineered specifically for wall, column and slab formwork. "H20" is not a brand name; it is a section designation used across Europe, the Middle East, and increasingly in India whenever a formwork specification calls for a certified timber beam. The most established manufacturer of the H20 is Form-on GmbH in Amstetten, Austria — Wallgreens is Form-on's authorised distributor for India.

Every H20 beam shares the same structural geometry: two solid softwood flanges (80 mm wide × 40 mm thick) bonded to a central web. Only the web material differs between variants. What makes the beam special is how far it spans for its weight — 4.4 to 5.2 kg/m carries a permissible bending moment of 5 kNm, which is why H20 has become the international default for anything above a small residential slab.

The EN 13377 Standard — What It Actually Means

EN 13377 is the European standard for prefabricated timber formwork beams. It specifies the beam's geometry, material grades, permitted defects in the timber, the manufacturing controls that must be in place, and — critically — the tested load capacities the beam is warranted to carry:

PropertyValueWhat It Means on Site
Permissible bending moment (M)5 kNmGoverns primary beam spacing under a slab
Permissible shear force (Q)11 kNGoverns prop spacing along the beam
Rigidity (E × J)450 kNm²Governs deflection under load — limited to l/500 mid-span
Section depth200 mmStandard H20 profile — interchangeable with any EN 13377 beam
Flange80 × 40 mmSolid softwood, machine stress-graded

When a consultant's drawing calls for "H20 to EN 13377," it means the site cannot substitute sawn timber, LVL, or an untested engineered beam. The certification is what makes the load tables downstream reliable — every combination of slab thickness, secondary spacing, primary spacing and prop spacing has been physically tested, not just calculated.

How Form-on Builds Its H20 Beams

  1. Flange sourcing — softwood is sourced from PEFC-certified Austrian forests, ensuring traceable, sustainably managed timber
  2. Stress grading — every flange is passed through a machine stress-grader; sub-grade timber is rejected before it enters the beam line
  3. Tensile testing — flanges are tensile-tested to verify the load-carrying capacity is what the certificate says it is
  4. Web preparation — the web is prepared per variant: three-ply panels for smartBEAM 20N, particle board for smartBEAM 20P and timberBEAM oP
  5. Bonding — the web is bonded into a routed groove in each flange using a certified structural adhesive
  6. End sealing — beam ends are sealed with a waterproof paint (yellow — the recognised H20 colour code across the industry)
  7. Marking — every beam is printed with the beam type, EN 13377 mark, manufacturing date, and a unique traceable serial code (visible as "FON..." on the flange)
  8. Cutting to standard lengths — beams are cut to ten standard lengths from 1.80 m to 5.90 m; five of these are held ex-stock at Wallgreens in India

The Three Form-on H20 Variants

All three variants share the same structural capacity (M 5 kNm · Q 11 kN · E×J 450 kNm²) and the same EN 13377 certification. They differ only in the web construction and — as a result — in weight and handling behaviour:

VariantWeb constructionWeightBest used for
Form-on smartBEAM 20NThree-ply web with finger-jointed splicesapprox. 4.4 kg/mWall and column formwork; sites where lower weight speeds manual handling of long lengths
Form-on smartBEAM 20PWood-composite particle board webapprox. 5.2 kg/mGeneral-purpose walls, columns and slab secondaries; continuous web load-path suits wall & column and gang-form work where heavy lifting matters
Form-on timberBEAM oPHomogeneous wood-composite pressboard webapprox. 4.9 kg/mSlab formwork as the primary and secondary driver; where high-repetition cycles and consistent capacity matter most

Practically, most Indian sites specify a single variant across the project so the store, the site engineer, and the carpenter all handle one beam. The 20N is the lightest and easiest to move manually; the oP is the heaviest but the most consistent under repeated slab loads.

Reading the Load Tables — A Worked Example

The load tables on the H20 product page follow a two-step logic that mirrors how a site engineer actually designs a slab table:

  1. Pick your slab thickness (e.g. 20 cm) — this fixes the concrete load per square metre (6.7 kN/m² for 20 cm)
  2. Pick your secondary beam spacing — this is usually driven by the plywood sheet format (0.500, 0.625, 0.667 or 0.750 m centres)
  3. Read the maximum permissible primary beam spacing off Table 1 (for 20 cm slab, 0.750 m secondaries → 2.61 m max primary spacing)
  4. Pick a primary spacing at or below that maximum (e.g. 2.50 m)
  5. Read the maximum permissible prop spacing off Table 2 (for 20 cm slab, 2.50 m primaries → 1.19 m max prop spacing)
  6. Compute props per 100 m² of slab: 1 / (primary × prop spacing) × 100 — in this example, roughly 34 props per 100 m²

All values assume EN 12812 conditions — a service load of 0.75 kN/m², a variable load of up to 10% of the slab weight, and a mid-span deflection limit of l/500. For anything unusual (post-tensioned decks, transfer slabs, unusually heavy pours), size the beam grid to a project-specific structural design rather than off the tabulated maxima.

H20 vs Conventional Sawn Timber

The reason H20 replaces sawn timber on almost every site that specifies it comes down to five practical differences:

CertificationEN 13377None — grade varies with each piece
Section depth200 mm — spans further, needs fewer props75 mm — needs closer prop spacing
Weight4.4–5.2 kg/m3.5–6 kg/m — depends on species and moisture
Load capacityConsistent along full lengthVariable — knots, grain, moisture content
Reuse cycles50+ cycles typical with proper handling3–5 cycles typical
Length availabilityTen standard lengths, 1.80 – 5.90 mLimited to sawmill dimensions
SustainabilityPEFC-certified sustainable forestryOften uncertified

H20 vs J70 StiMBERTM — Complementary, Not Competing

Wallgreens offers both the Form-on H20 (imported from Austria) and the India-made J70 StiMBERTM (manufactured in Morbi). They are different tools for different jobs — not substitutes:

  • Use H20 for primary beams and long spans. Its 450 kNm² rigidity is roughly seven times J70's — the deeper section wins wherever spans are longer than about 2 m or when a spec calls out EN 13377 explicitly.
  • Use J70 StiMBERTM for secondary beams and abrasive site conditions. The hot-dip galvanised steel channel and timber filler shrug off water, impact and rough handling in a way an all-timber beam cannot. On wet or coastal sites, J70 outlasts H20 in the same rotation.
  • Most Indian projects use both. A typical arrangement: H20 primaries at 2.5 m centres, J70 secondaries at 0.5 m centres, phenolic plywood on top. Talk to our engineers if you'd like a table layout for a specific slab.

How to Specify H20 in a Bill of Quantities

  • Beam type — "H20 timber formwork beam to EN 13377, from Form-on GmbH (Austria) or approved equivalent"
  • Variant — specify oP, smartBEAM 20N, or smartBEAM 20P; if left open, Wallgreens will supply the variant best suited to your predominant use case
  • Lengths — quote the lengths you actually need; ex-stock lengths in India are 2.45 m, 2.90 m, 3.60 m, 4.50 m and 4.90 m (48-hour dispatch). Other lengths from 1.80 to 5.90 m are supplied on indent from Austria (typically 8–10 weeks)
  • Quantity — supplied in bundles (100 pieces for lengths up to 4.90 m; 60 pieces for 5.90 m)
  • Basis — H20 is a purchase-only product; unlike J70 and Mach Deck, it is not on the Wallgreens rental fleet
